Anthropic and Microsoft Open Talks for the Maia 200 Chip, the First Cross Cloud Silicon Deal of the AI Era

Anthropic is in early talks with Microsoft to deploy Claude on Microsoft's custom Maia 200 AI accelerator, a deal first reported by The Information on May 21 and substantiated by CNBC, Bloomberg, and Reuters. Microsoft says Maia 200 delivers more than 30% better tokens per dollar than the latest silicon in its fleet, and Anthropic has openly said it has "difficulties with compute" while paying SpaceX $1.25B per month through May 2029. If signed, it would be the first time a frontier lab runs production traffic on a rival cloud provider's custom chip, after Anthropic already locked AWS Trainium and Google TPU commitments worth more than $100B combined. The strategic read is that the compute floor is now sized in trillions of dollars, and any frontier lab that wants to stay frontier has to court every silicon vendor on the planet.

Leaked audio from an April 30 Meta all hands surfaced this week, with Mark Zuckerberg explicitly defending an internal surveillance program called the "Model Capability Initiative" that captured keystrokes, mouse activity, and periodic screenshots from employee company devices across Gmail, GChat, the Metamate assistant, and VSCode. Gemini 3.5 Flash Becomes Google's New Default Across Gemini App and AI Mode in Search

Google promoted Gemini 3.5 Flash to the default model for both the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, globally. Google says the model outperforms Gemini 3.1 Pro on Terminal Bench 2.1 (76.2%), GDPval AA (1656 Elo), and MCP Atlas (83.6%), while running about four times the speed of comparable frontier models at less than half the cost. The pitch is clear: Google is no longer optimizing Flash for "fast but cheap," it is optimizing it for agentic long horizon workflows where a smaller, faster model gets to act, not just answer.

Gemini Spark Ships with MCP Connectors for Canva, Instacart, and OpenTable on Day One

Google's new 24/7 personal AI agent, Gemini Spark, launched at I/O 2026 with native MCP support and day one connectors for Canva, OpenTable, and Instacart, with Adobe, Samsung, Spotify, and CapCut arriving over the summer. The big read is not the agent itself but the protocol choice: by standardizing on MCP (the open standard Anthropic introduced in November 2024), Google is conceding that the agent stack is multi vendor and that the interop layer has effectively been decided. Spark runs persistently on dedicated Google Cloud VMs and asks for approval before high stakes actions like spending money or sending external email.
