OpenAI Preps GPT-5.6 for Launch Next Week — 1.5M Token Context, Faster Codex, and Pricing Built to Undercut Anthropic

OpenAI is preparing to ship GPT-5.6 as soon as next week, potentially in Mini and Pro variants, with references already surfacing in Codex backend logs. The headline upgrades are a ~1.5 million token context window (up roughly 43% from GPT-5.5's 1.05M), improved long-horizon coding, and noticeably faster Codex response times. Pricing is explicitly aimed at undercutting Anthropic — timing that lands while US export-control issues have knocked Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 offline. OpenAI's chief scientist has called it a "meaningful" leap, though no official model card, benchmarks, or pricing have been published yet, so treat the spec sheet as developer-log and community sourced until launch day.

#2

Midjourney's Wild Hardware Pivot: A Full-Body Ultrasonic Scanner — Housed Inside Its Own Spas

The AI image generator Midjourney revealed its most surprising launch yet: the Midjourney Scanner, a full-body ultrasonic imaging machine that lowers users through a ring of ultrasound sensors and produces an MRI-grade 3D body map in about 60 seconds, versus the 60-90 minutes a conventional MRI takes. Founder David Holz says it was built with ultrasound-chip maker Butterfly Network, packs 8,960 transducers processing ~17 GB/second, and is designed to cost a few dollars per scan. The rollout is unusual too — the first Midjourney Spa opens in San Francisco's Union Square in 2027, pairing roughly 10 scanners with saunas and cold plunges, with a stated long-term goal of a billion scans a month by 2031. The engineering demo is striking; the clinical, regulatory, and reimbursement case remains almost entirely unproven.

#3

Anthropic Says It's "Confident" of Restoring Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 Within Days, After a US Export-Control Order Pulled Them Offline

An Anthropic executive said this week the company is confident it can re-enable access to its flagship Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models "in coming days," after a June 12 US government export-control directive forced it to disable both for all customers. The order required Anthropic to suspend access for foreign nationals — inside or outside the US, including its own foreign-national employees — over concerns that Fable 5's safeguards against cybersecurity tasks like vulnerability discovery could be jailbroken. Anthropic complied by cutting access for everyone rather than building geo-gating overnight, leaving Opus 4.8 as the top generally available model in the interim. It's the clearest example yet of a government directly reaching into a frontier lab's deployment — and a live test of how fast a lab can come back from one.

Perplexity shipped Brain, a memory system that builds a persistent context graph across tasks, projects, decisions, files, and sources so agents start with relevant context instead of from scratch.
